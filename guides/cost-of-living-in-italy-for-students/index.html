Quick answer: housing usually changes the budget most

A student's final cost depends heavily on the city, room type, distance from campus and whether meals or accommodation are supported by a regional benefit. Milan and central tourist areas normally require a larger housing allowance than smaller university cities.

Create a low, expected and high estimate. Do not count a scholarship until its amount, payment timing and renewal conditions are confirmed in the current call.

Separate monthly costs from arrival costs

Monthly planning normally covers rent, utilities, food, local transport, phone, study materials and personal spending. Arrival planning includes travel, visa-related expenses, housing deposits, initial rent, residence-permit costs and items needed before the first scholarship payment.

Keeping the two budgets separate prevents a student from appearing able to afford the year while lacking the cash needed during the first weeks.

  • Recurring: rent and utilities
  • Recurring: food and transport
  • Recurring: phone, health and study costs
  • One-time: visa and travel
  • One-time: deposit and first rent
  • One-time: residence permit and setup costs

Build a city-specific housing plan

Compare university residences, regional accommodation, shared rooms, private rooms and studio apartments. Ask whether utilities, internet, condominium charges or registration fees are included.

Never transfer a deposit only because a social-media advertiser creates urgency. Verify the property, contract, owner or authorised agent and cancellation terms. University housing offices can provide safer starting points but availability is limited.

Food, transport and everyday costs

Cooking at home, using student canteens and buying a student transport pass can reduce recurring costs. Your campus location matters: cheaper rent outside the city centre may create higher transport costs and longer travel time.

Add a buffer for seasonal utilities, course materials, clothing, health expenses and trips required for documents or appointments.

How scholarships change the cash-flow plan

A tuition waiver reduces university cost but may not pay rent or food. A regional benefit can include cash, meals or accommodation, but payment dates and student-status rules matter as much as the advertised value.

Calculate how much money is needed before the first confirmed payment. Keep a contingency amount and avoid building the budget around work income that is not yet secured.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to live in Italy as a student?+

There is no single amount. Housing, city, lifestyle and scholarship services produce large differences, so build a city-specific monthly and arrival budget.

Is Milan more expensive for students?+

Housing demand often makes Milan a higher-budget destination than many smaller cities, but the actual room, commute and scholarship support determine the final cost.

Does a DSU scholarship cover all living costs?+

Not automatically. Benefits and payment timing vary by region, student status and current call. Compare cash, meals, accommodation and uncovered costs separately.

Should I include part-time work in my budget?+

Treat unconfirmed work income as a possible later benefit, not as the money required for visa, travel or the first months.
